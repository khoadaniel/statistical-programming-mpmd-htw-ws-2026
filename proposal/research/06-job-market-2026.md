# Research notes — What gets graduates hired into data/ML/AI jobs (Germany/Berlin, 2026)

Research date: 2026-09-24.

**Evidence labels:**
- **[S]** strong: primary source, large sample
- **[M]** moderate
- **[W]** weak or indicative

PwC primary pages returned 403, so their numbers come from snippets and secondary summaries.

## 1. The market: two markets at once
**Specialist shortage persists:**
- **Bitkom (3 Sep 2026):** 79k IT vacancies, down from 109k in 2025 and 149k in 2023. 78 % of firms still report a shortage. Missing German is the #1 hiring barrier (47 %). [S]

**Entry-level collapse:**
- **Indeed Hiring Lab DE (23 Sep 2026):** entry-level postings down **46 % vs 2019**. [S]
  - Software development down ~70 %.
  - IT applications down 77 %.
  - **Project management entry-level down 60 %.**
  - Occupations with more than 60 % AI-automatable skills are at about half their 2019 entry-level volume.
- **Bundesagentur für Arbeit:** ~54k unemployed ICT professionals (+27 %, a 10-year high); software development is no longer a shortage occupation. [S]
- **Stanford "Canaries in the Coal Mine" (updated Aug 2026):** employment for 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs is ~19 % below trend. The effect comes from less hiring, not layoffs, and is concentrated where AI *automates* rather than *augments*. [S]
- **SignalFire 2026:** new-grad hiring down ~65 % at large tech firms and ~76 % at startups vs 2019. [M]

**Where AI demand is:**
- **Indeed (Jul 2026):** 4.2 % of German postings are AI-touched, the highest among its European peers; **59 % of these are outside tech occupations**. [S]
- **PwC 2026 DE:** 125k AI ads in 2025, at a ratio of **7 AI-user roles to 1 AI-developer role**; AI skills carry a 20 %+ premium (39 % in energy). [S/snippet]
- **Lightcast:** 28 % salary premium for AI skills; 51 % of AI postings are outside IT. [S]
- **WEF Future of Jobs 2025:** "AI and big data" is the fastest-growing skill. [S]

## 4. How candidates are evaluated
- **AI-enabled coding interviews:** Meta (since Oct 2025) scores problem solving, code quality, **verification** and communication; Canva expects candidates to use AI tools. [M]
- **Karat 2026:** 71 % of engineering leaders say AI makes technical skills harder to assess, so take-homes are losing credibility. [M]
- **In-person rounds are returning** (Google, McKinsey). [M]
- **Data-scientist interview loops:** SQL screen → statistics/ML → case or take-home. **A/B testing, statistics, ML and product sense make up more than 55 % of questions.** [W–M]
- **ML/AI-engineer loops:** coding (often AI-enabled) + ML/LLM system design + project deep dive + **eval design**. [M]
- **What makes juniors stand out:** proof of work (shipped apps, open source, agentic workflows), a deployed demo instead of a notebook, and a **live, explainable walkthrough of their own repo**. [M]

## 5. Germany specifics
- **German language:** 36 % of Berlin data ads ask for it (data engineering 38 %, analyst 37 %). Consultancies, the public sector and the Mittelstand usually require it. **B1–B2 roughly doubles the addressable market.**
- **Blue Card 2026:** the threshold is €50,700, or **€45,934.20 for IT and for graduates within 3 years of their degree**. **§20 AufenthG** gives an 18-month job-search permit. Students may work 140 full / 280 half days a year.
- **Salaries:**
  - Junior data scientist in Berlin: ~€55k (Glassdoor), €51.8–62.1k (get in IT).
  - Entry AI/ML engineer: €55–68k.
  - ML engineer, all levels: median total compensation €93k (Levels.fyi).
- **Sectors hiring in Berlin:** energy/climate tech (1KOMMA5°, Reonic; highest AI premium), consulting (Deloitte, EY, Capgemini), fintech, SaaS/e-commerce, health; GovTech requires German.
- **EU AI Act:** Art. 4 (AI literacy) was softened by the Digital Omnibus (Reg. 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026) to "take measures to support". It is a differentiator in consulting, public sector, health and fintech, not a hiring filter.

## 7. What a hireable portfolio project looks like in 2026
1. A real question and a real user, ideally in a German sector.
2. End to end: SQL/dbt modelling → model or LLM → public URL.
3. An **explicit eval section**: baseline, held-out set, error analysis; for LLM features, judge-vs-human agreement and cost per request.
4. **A statistical decision**: an experiment or quasi-experiment, with uncertainty.
5. Engineering hygiene, and the ability to do a live walkthrough **without AI**.
6. A one-page stakeholder memo, a 5-minute demo video, and a responsible-AI note.
7. One strong project beats five Kaggle notebooks.

## 8. Assessment that mirrors real hiring
- Portfolio project, hiring-style rubric: 40 %
- **Live SQL + pandas practical without AI**: 15 %
- Take-home with AI allowed + 10-minute oral defence: 20 %
- **System design review** (30 min): 15 %
- Stakeholder pitch + PR peer reviews: 10 %
